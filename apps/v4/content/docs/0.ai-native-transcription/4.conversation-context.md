Conversation meaning rarely fits inside a single sentence. A name introduced in the first minute, a product architecture under discussion, or a technical term from three turns ago determines how the current utterance should be understood.

## Acoustic window vs. human-like semantic context

One of the sharpest differences between conventional speech recognition and Lanson Live is **what "context" means**:

- **An ASR model's context is acoustic and local**: Even the best speech recognition models operate over a narrow sliding window of audio frames and immediate token transitions. Once an utterance ends, the acoustic decoder moves on. It does not hold a semantic mental model of the conversation.
- **Lanson Live's reasoning model has human-like semantic context**: Because [Context-aware Refinement](/docs/ai-native-transcription/context-aware-refinement) uses a separate language reasoning model, it evaluates each new ASR candidate from the same perspective a human listener has—knowing *who* is speaking, *what topic* has been discussed over prior turns, and *which entities or technical terms* are active in the session.

## Solving the short-window paradox of real-time speech

In offline batch transcription, an AI reviewer can scan an entire 60-minute recording before deciding how to spell an ambiguous proper noun in minute two.

In real-time transcription, you cannot wait 60 minutes—you only have the current segment collection window (often just `3–8 seconds` of audio). If the pipeline treated every 4-second segment in isolation, real-time review would fail whenever a speaker said a brief follow-up sentence like:

> *"Check the LiveFinal latency metrics."*

To an acoustic model looking only at 2 seconds of sound, `"LiveFinal"` is acoustically indistinguishable from `"life final"` or `"live vinyl"`.

To Lanson Live's reasoning model—which knows that two turns earlier the speaker said *"We're deploying LiveFinal on the realtime gateway today"*—the meaning is unmistakable.

## How cross-utterance memory works in the pipeline

Lanson Live pairs **intra-segment windowing** ([StableStream™](/docs/ai-native-transcription/stablestream)) with **inter-segment conversational memory**:

1. **Rolling semantic memory**: Completed utterances—including ultra-short turns that required no modification themselves—accumulate into a rolling session context window fed to the reasoning model.
2. **Entity and terminology anchoring**: Once a proper noun, product name, or domain term appears in the conversation, subsequent phonetic lookalikes are evaluated against that established semantic anchor.
3. **Bounded real-time overhead**: Session context is maintained in a distilled rolling window (and an evolving session guide in the `pro` profile) so that multi-turn semantic comprehension never causes streaming latency to grow as a conversation runs.

| Context Dimension | Model Layer | What It Captures |
|---|---|---|
| **Acoustic & local window** | Layer 1: ASR Recognition Model | Phonemes, prosody, and immediate word transitions inside the current audio slice. |
| **Intra-segment window** | Layer 3: `StableStream™` Buffer | Complete clause or sentence boundaries collected before utterance finalization. |
| **Multi-turn semantic memory** | Layer 2: Dedicated Reasoning Model | Human-like comprehension of prior turns, recurring entities, domain terminology, and conversational meaning. |
